Gaps this data can't show yet

Events with damage but no declaration, counties whose preliminary damage assessment met the threshold but were left out, and county resource requests to the state. Adding damage assessment records and resource requests would fill these in, and would let the model learn from events that didn't escalate.

How this was worked out, and what to keep in mind
  • Data. FEMA declaration and Public Assistance project records from the GRRF workbook. Only categories A and B are included, so totals are a floor on PA, not the full award.
  • Window. Declarations from January 1999, when the workbook's PA project records start, through November 18, 2026.
  • Net, gross, deobligated. Each project version is a row. Positive versions add up to gross obligations, negative versions to deobligations, and the sum of all versions is net.
  • Threshold. FEMA countywide per capita indicator for the fiscal year × 2020 census population. Indicator values come from FEMA's published table (FY2004–FY2025), Federal Register notices (FY2002, FY2003, FY2027) and FEMA's FY2026 notice. From FY2026, the indicator applies by incident start date. FEMA used older census counts for earlier years, so older multiples are approximate.
  • Five-year chance. 1 − e−5λ, where λ is the county's PA major-disaster designations per year since 1999.
  • Escalation. An emergency declaration counts as escalated when a major declaration shares its incident ID, or covers an overlapping incident period within 120 days and shares counties.
  • $0 in A + B. Counties designated for PA with no net A or B dollars. They may have had projects in categories C–G. Declarations under 2 years old are left out because obligations are still being written.
  • Hazard filter. Scores, chances and dollars are worked out again on the page from the county designations for the hazard picked. "Emergency declaration, left out of the major" stays all hazards.
  • Map. County boundaries are U.S. Census county outlines (via the plotly/datasets GeoJSON), matched to the workbook's county names.
  • County of applicant. 14 applicants had no county in the source and were assigned to their home county. Two out-of-state applicants stay unassigned.
